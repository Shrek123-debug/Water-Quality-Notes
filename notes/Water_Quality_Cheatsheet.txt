1. WATER, SALT, DENSITY: H2O polar (105 degrees; O delta-, H delta+) -> hydrogen bonds -> high heat capacity (oceans buffer climate; marine organisms avoid temp swings), highest latent heats, high boiling/melting points (without H-bonds water would boil ~-80C), cohesion (surface tension: water striders), adhesion + capillarity, universal solvent (ions get hydration shells), only substance naturally solid/liquid/gas. Max density of fresh water 4C; ice (0.917 g/cm3, hexagonal lattice) floats and insulates (lakes freeze top-down; turnover spring/fall). Seawater 96.5% water + 3.5% salts: NaCl ~85% of salts (3.0% Na+Cl, 0.5% others). Ions (g/kg at 35 ppt): Cl 19.35 (55%), Na 10.75 (30.6%), SO4 2.70 (7.7%), Mg 1.30 (3.7%), Ca 0.42 (1.2%), K 0.39 (1.1%) = six ions >99%; then HCO3 0.145, Br 0.066, borate, Sr 0.013, F. Rule of CONSTANT PROPORTIONS (Dittmar): major-ion ratios fixed -> measure one (Cl or conductivity) to get salinity; salinity changes by adding/removing pure water. S(ppt) = 1.80655 x Cl(ppt) (= 0.0018066 x Cl mg/L; 18,740 mg/kg Cl -> 33.9 ppt; Knudsen S = 0.03 + 1.805 Cl). Salts come from rock weathering/river runoff (cations) + hydrothermal vents/volcanoes (Cl-, sulfate); major ions last millions of yr, trace metals (Fe) ~100 yr. Open ocean 33-37 ppt; Red Sea ~40; Baltic surface ~7; Black Sea ~18; brine >50; evaporating pools >100. Salinity DOWN: precipitation, runoff, melting ice; UP: evaporation, sea-ice formation (brine rejection). Salinity classes (Venice): fresh <0.5; oligohaline 0.5-5; mesohaline 5-18; polyhaline 18-30; euhaline 30-40; hyperhaline >40. Density: salinity UP = density UP, temperature UP = density DOWN; seawater ~1.025 g/cm3 (1025 kg/m3; sigma-t = rho - 1000 ~ 25; range 1.023-1.029), fresh 1.000; temperature controls density more than salinity in most open ocean (polar/estuary salinity matters more); a 1C change ~ 0.1 salinity change in density; seawater freezes ~-1.9C (35 ppt); for S >24.7 ppt max-density temp is BELOW freezing (so oceans convect to the bottom, no lake-style lid), for S <24.7 max density >freezing. Conductivity rises with salinity AND temperature (~2%/C); salinometer/CTD = conductivity-temperature-depth; practical salinity is a ratio (no units). Dissolved gases dissolve BETTER in COLD water: O2 ~5-9 mg/L (0-8.5 mL/L, air 21% = 210,000 ppm), CO2 far more soluble (reacts) so ocean holds >50x atmosphere's CO2; O2 from photosynthesis + air exchange; O2-minimum zone ~500-1000 m (decay, no photosynthesis). Pressure +1 atm per 10 m (P = 1 + depth/10 atm); gas spaces compress at depth (swim bladders expand if hauled up fast). Light: red absorbed first, blue-green deepest in clear open ocean, green in turbid coastal water; euphotic zone ~100-200 m (clearest water 1% light ~220 m); extinction coefficient k = 1.7/Secchi depth (or 1.45/SD); photic depth ~ 2-3 x Secchi depth.

2. OCEAN ZONES, CIRCULATION, CLIMATE: Pelagic (water column) vs benthic (bottom: substrate, benthos; infauna in, epifauna on; demersal = near bottom; meiobenthos <0.5 mm). Pelagic horizontal: NERITIC (over continental shelf, <200 m, ~8% of ocean, most fisheries, high productivity, shelf break avg ~150 m) vs OCEANIC (beyond shelf). Intertidal (littoral) = between tide marks (only zone exposed to air); subtidal. Vertical: epipelagic 0-200 m (euphotic: light enough for photosynthesis) > mesopelagic 200-1000 (disphotic/twilight; main thermocline; oxygen minimum; photophores) > bathypelagic 1000-4000 (aphotic) > abyssopelagic 4000-6000 > hadopelagic >6000 (trenches); benthic: bathyal (to 4000 m), abyssal (4000-6000), hadal. Plankton drift (phytoplankton producers: diatoms = silica frustule, cold/nutrient-rich; dinoflagellates = warm, 2 flagella, red tides; coccolithophores CaCO3; cyanobacteria Prochlorococcus/Trichodesmium N-fixer; zooplankton: copepods ~70% of net zooplankton, krill (Euphausia superba, 6 cm, Antarctic key prey), larvaceans; holoplankton whole life vs meroplankton larvae; nekton swim; neuston at surface); size classes: pico <2 um, nano 2-20, micro 20-200, meso 0.2-20 mm; phytoplankton ~50% of Earth's primary production and O2. Phytoplankton blooms need light + nutrients: spring bloom (stratification + light), temperate fall bloom smaller; tropics permanently stratified/low steady; polar light-limited; critical depth/compensation depth (photosynthesis = respiration); self-shading. Primary production (g C/m2/yr): coral reefs 1500-3700 (highest), kelp 640-1800, seagrass 550-1100, salt marsh 250-2000, mangrove 370-450, estuaries (shallow) high, coastal upwelling 110-370, open gyres 4-40 (ocean deserts), Arctic <1-100; rain forest 460-1600. Light/dark bottle: light = net, dark = respiration, GPP = net + resp; 14C method. Winds: trades (0-30 deg, NE/SE, steady), westerlies 30-60, polar easterlies; Coriolis deflects RIGHT in N, LEFT in S (zero at equator); Ekman: surface water 45 deg from wind, net transport 90 deg (right N); gyres clockwise N, counter-clockwise S; warm WESTERN boundary currents (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio, Brazil, Agulhas), cold EASTERN (Canary, California, Benguela, Peru/Humboldt, W Australia); corals extend poleward on west sides of oceans, kelp equatorward on east sides. Upwelling (cold, nutrient-rich, high productivity: coastal eastern boundaries, equatorial divergence, Antarctic; Peru anchoveta = world's biggest fishery); downwelling = onshore Ekman. El Nino: weak trades, warm nutrient-poor water suppresses Peru upwelling (fish/seabird crash, floods Peru, drought Indonesia/Australia, global coral bleaching: 1982-83, 1997-98); La Nina opposite; ENSO = El Nino-Southern Oscillation (Darwin vs Tahiti pressure). Thermohaline "conveyor": NADW sinks near Greenland/Labrador, AABW near Weddell/Ross seas, ~1000-yr cycles, carries O2 to deep; sinking water must be cold + salty. Stratification: mixed layer 50-150 m > thermocline (temp), halocline (salinity), pycnocline (density); tropics permanent strong thermocline, mid-lat seasonal, polar none; stable = light over dense. Waves: crest, trough, height, wavelength, period; particles move in circles, energy not water moves; size = wind speed x duration x FETCH; tsunami = seismic sea wave (not tidal; >700 km/h, <1 m in open ocean; mangroves/reefs reduce damage). Tides: moon+sun gravity + rotation; 2 highs/2 lows per 24 h 50 min; SPRING (large range) at new & full moon; NEAP (small) at quarters; semidiurnal (US east coast), mixed (US west), diurnal (Gulf of Mexico); drive estuary circulation, trigger spawning. FIVE OCEAN GARBAGE PATCHES = subtropical gyre accumulation zones: North Pacific (Great Pacific Garbage Patch between Hawaii & California ~1.6 million km2, largest), South Pacific, North Atlantic, South Atlantic, Indian (NOT a "Central Pacific"); mostly microplastics <5 mm dispersed from surface to depth (not a solid island), ghost nets (fishing gear largest mass), harms = ingestion/false satiety, entanglement, rafting invasives, toxin sorption (PCBs, DDT) biomagnifies; bycatch = unintended catch.
3. ESTUARIES: Estuary = partly enclosed coastal body where river/stream fresh water meets and mixes with ocean water (bays, lagoons, sounds, sloughs; Chesapeake = largest N. American; freshwater estuaries exist where rivers meet Great Lakes = storm/seiche-driven, not tidal); salinity varies from ~0 to ocean and daily with tides; among most productive and most threatened ecosystems; 22 of 32 largest cities on estuaries; ~3/4 of US commercial catch (and most fish/shellfish eaten: salmon, herring, oysters) use estuaries as nursery ("nurseries of the sea"). BY CIRCULATION (set by river-flow R vs tidal volume V; Ss:Sb salinity ratio): SALT WEDGE (strongly stratified; river dominates; sharp halocline, thin fresh layer over landward-creeping wedge; Mississippi, Columbia, Hudson, Congo; bottom hypoxia); HIGHLY STRATIFIED/FJORD-type (deep U-shaped basin + shallow SILL, high river flow, little tidal mixing; entrainment only upward; stagnant, anoxic bottom water behind sill; BC, Alaska, Chile, NZ, Norway); SLIGHTLY STRATIFIED/PARTIALLY MIXED (tides erase wedge: salt mixed up and fresh mixed down; surface still fresher than bottom; Puget Sound, SF Bay, Chesapeake); VERTICALLY MIXED/WELL MIXED (strong tides, low river flow, shallow; same salinity top to bottom, rises toward sea; Delaware Bay); INVERSE/NEGATIVE (arid, evaporation > freshwater, hypersaline: salinity rises toward head, dense water sinks, bottom outflow; Laguna Madre >50 ppt); INTERMITTENT (dries up in dry season). BY GEOLOGY: COASTAL PLAIN/drowned river valley (sea-level rise after ice melted ~18,000 yr ago; most common; Chesapeake, Delaware; V-shaped); BAR-BUILT (barrier islands/sandbars wall off lagoon; Outer Banks, Texas coast, Pamlico Sound); FJORD (glacier-carved, sill); TECTONIC (land subsides along faults; San Francisco Bay, Manukau Harbour); DELTAIC (sediment-built at river mouths; bird-foot Mississippi, cuspate wave-dominated Nile, tide-dominated Fly River); RIA (drowned, wide inlets). Passive margins (Atlantic coast) = many estuaries; active margins (Pacific coast) = few. Physical facts: Coriolis deflects outflow right (N) so marine water penetrates farther on left side; salt flocculation (clay particles clump in brackish water and sink -> muddy estuary, turbidity maximum at halocline where fresh meets salt); sand settles upstream, mud downstream; estuarine mud anoxic below few cm (black, H2S "rotten egg"); flushing/residence time = V/net outflow; tidal bore. HABITATS: salt marsh (Spartina cordgrass seaward -> S. patens, Juncus, Salicornia pickleweed -> Distichlis; cordgrass excretes salt via glands, roots trap sediment; salt pannes), mangroves (tropical/subtropical, 40N-40S; red = seaward prop roots, black = pneumatophores + leaf salt glands, white = landward; viviparous seedlings; Indo-W Pacific most diverse), seagrass beds/SAV (Zostera eelgrass temperate, Thalassia turtle grass tropical; true flowering plants with roots; light-limited; nursery, sediment stabilizer; wasting disease Labyrinthula), oyster reefs (Crassostrea virginica; brackish <10 m; filter water), mudflats (diatoms/bacteria produce most; burrowers; shorebird bills partition prey by depth), sand flats, tidal streams/creeks, barrier beaches, kelp forests (cold, nutrient-rich, 15-40 m, rocky), rocky/soft shorelines, deepwater swamps/riverine forests; NOT estuarine: coral reefs. Producers: phytoplankton, benthic diatoms, algae, seagrass, marsh plants (unlike open ocean = phytoplankton only); food comes mostly via DETRITUS (bacteria/fungi decompose dead marsh plants; detritivores, deposit/filter feeders: clams, oysters, mussels, worms; outwelling exports nutrients); top predators fish, birds, humans. ADAPTATIONS to salinity/mud/waves/tides: euryhaline (wide tolerance) vs stenohaline; osmoconformers (mussels, polychaetes; internal salt follows water) vs osmoregulators (fish, crabs: gills/kidney/active transport; marine fish drink seawater and excrete salt via gills, freshwater fish do not drink and gain water); close shells/valves, burrow, move out, excrete salt (salt glands), succulence (pickleweed), strong/crowded roots, pneumatophores, increase respiratory flow, high-affinity hemoglobin. Species richness lowest at mid-salinity (5-10 ppt). IMPORTANCE: filters sediment/pollutants/nutrients, buffers storms/floods/erosion, nurseries, migratory-bird stopovers, fisheries, recreation, jobs. THREATS: coastal development/filling/dredging (1/3 of US estuaries lost), nutrient pollution -> eutrophication/hypoxia/HABs, sedimentation, overfishing, dams/diversions (cut freshwater/sediment), invasive species, toxins, sea-level rise/climate; most pollutants are land-based/nonpoint. INVASIVE/HARMFUL SPECIES (non-native, spread by ballast water, hull fouling, aquaculture, aquarium release; ~42% of threatened/endangered spp. at risk; $137 billion/yr US; "invasion meltdown"): zebra/quagga mussels (Great Lakes), Chinese mitten crab (catadromous, burrows), European green crab, Asian clam (SF Bay), comb jelly Mnemiopsis (Black Sea collapse), Spartina alterniflora (Pacific, hybridizes), Caulerpa taxifolia, lionfish (Atlantic); control = ballast-water exchange/treatment, bans. NERRS = National Estuarine Research Reserve System (NOAA + states: research, monitoring, education, stewardship; ~30 reserves, >1.3 million acres). Estuary synonyms: bay, lagoon, sound, slough, inlet, firth, harbor, fjord, tidal creek, river mouth. Mississippi: drains >40% of continental US -> N + P -> Gulf dead zone (~4,000+ sq mi; avg 15,600 km2 2000-05).

4. FOOD WEBS, COMMUNITIES, POPULATIONS, LIFE HISTORY: Levels: population (one species) < community (all species) < ecosystem (+abiotic) < biosphere; abiotic factors: light, temperature, salinity, pH, pressure, dissolved gases, nutrients, tides, currents, waves, substrate; biotic: predation, competition, symbiosis, disease. Habitat = where; niche = role (fundamental = potential without competitors; realized = actual). Producers (photo-/chemo-autotrophs) > primary consumers (herbivores; trophic level 2) > secondary (1st-order carnivores) > tertiary > quaternary; decomposers/detritivores/scavengers; omnivores feed at several levels; food chain = one path, web = many. ~10% rule (5-20%): only ~10% of energy moves up (herbivore efficiency ~20%, carnivores 10-15%); 80-90% lost as respiration/heat/waste -> few trophic levels, few top predators; 50,000 J producers -> 5,000 -> 500 (secondary consumers); 76,000,000 J -> 2 steps -> 760 kJ; energy flows one way, nutrients cycle; pyramids of energy never invert, biomass/numbers can invert in the sea (fast phytoplankton turnover, small standing stock). GPP (total) - respiration = NPP; standing stock vs productivity; GCE; DDT biomagnification (0.1 -> 0.3 -> 0.5 -> 2 -> 10-25 ppm; fat-soluble, persistent), bioaccumulation (one organism over time), bioconcentration (from water). Trophic cascade (top-down): overfish seals/sea lions -> orcas eat sea otters -> urchins boom -> kelp lost; overfish groupers/sharks -> parrotfish decline -> algae overgrow reef; bottom-up = nutrients drive producers. Keystone species (low abundance, large effect: sea otter, triton, Pisaster sea star); foundation (corals, mangroves, kelp); dominant (abundant). Interactions: competition (-/-; intraspecific vs interspecific; competitive exclusion; resource partitioning; interference vs exploitation), predation (+/-), parasitism (+/-), mutualism (+/+: coral-zooxanthellae, cleaner shrimp-fish, tube worm-bacteria), commensalism (+/0: barnacle on whale, emperor shrimp on sea cucumber), amensalism (0/-), neutralism (0/0), facultative vs obligate symbiosis; coevolution (arms race); intermediate disturbance hypothesis (max diversity at moderate disturbance). Population: size, density, distribution (clumped most common, uniform = territoriality, random), age structure (pre-reproductive/reproductive/post: pyramid wide base = growing, even = stable, narrow base = declining), natality/mortality/immigration/emigration; exponential J: dN/dt = rN, doubling = ln2/r; logistic S: dN/dt = rN(K-N)/K, K = carrying capacity, MSY = rK/4 at N = K/2, growth fastest at K/2; limiting factors: density-dependent (food, space, disease, predators) vs density-independent (storms, temperature, floods); biotic potential = max under ideal conditions; Allee effect (low density hurts fertilization). Life-history strategies: r-selected (unstable/new habitats; pioneers in primary succession; many small young, early maturity, short life, little parental care, Type III survivorship, density-independent; oysters, urchins, crown-of-thorns, fleshy algae, dandelion, insects, salmon [semelparous]) vs K-selected (stable, near K; few large young, parental care, long life, late maturity; humans, whales, elephants, corals, sharks, groupers, giant clam, redwoods); survivorship Type I (low early mortality, late death: humans, whales), Type II (constant: birds, hydra, squirrels), Type III (high juvenile mortality: oysters, fish, plants, turtles). Life table: nx (alive at start of age x), lx = nx/n0, dx = nx - nx+1, qx = dx/nx, mx = offspring per female, R0 = Sigma lx*mx (>1 growing, =1 stable, <1 declining; rabbit example R0 = 1.00); fecundity = offspring per individual; semelparous (once) vs iteroparous (repeated; bet-hedging). Succession: primary (bare rock/new lava/glacier retreat; pioneers) vs secondary (after disturbance; soil present); pioneer -> transitional -> climax; reef/rocky shore: film -> algae -> barnacles -> mussels climax; coral reefs: coral -> disturbed -> turf/macroalgae phase shift (hard to reverse). Biodiversity: richness (# species), abundance (# individuals), evenness; Shannon index; highest in tropics/reefs; Jaccard J = shared/total species (a/(a+b+c)). Functional responses (Holling): I linear; II hyperbolic (handling time); III sigmoid (refuge/learning). Adaptation = heritable; acclimation isn't. Watersheds: land draining to a water body (divide/ridge, basin, endorheic = closed); management reduces flooding, improves quality, protects habitat; threats: urbanization/impervious surfaces, farming, mining, logging, dams; point (pipe, factory, WWTP, tanker spill) vs nonpoint (runoff: farms, roads, lawns, construction, atmospheric deposition, septic; majority, harder to control). TEK (Traditional Ecological Knowledge): place-based knowledge, practices and beliefs of Indigenous/local peoples built over generations (observation, oral transmission, stewardship, reciprocity) used with science: Hawaiian ahupuaa watershed management + loko iai fishponds + kapu seasonal bans; Pacific Northwest clam gardens, salmon first-fruits rites and tidal-calendar knowledge (knew red tides poison mussels); Inuit/ArcticNet sea-ice and hunting observations; Pacific locally managed marine areas (LMMAs) and tabu closures; Maori rahui (temporary closure); Aboriginal sea country; in scenarios: TEK + data = co-management, monitoring baselines, species indicators, ciguatera local knowledge.
5. WATER & NUTRIENT CYCLES: WATER: 97% salt (ocean ~97.5%), 3% fresh = 2% ice + ~1% liquid usable (groundwater largest liquid fresh store; lakes/rivers tiny); processes: precipitation, evaporation, transpiration (plants, via leaves), evapotranspiration, sublimation, condensation, interception (canopy), infiltration (reduced by impervious surfaces), percolation, runoff/snowmelt, groundwater flow; Sun-driven; ocean evaporation > precipitation on ocean, runoff returns the difference; residence time longest in oceans/ice. CARBON: reservoirs (GT): marine sediment/rock ~1,000,000+ (largest), deep ocean 38,000, fossil fuels ~12,000, soil 1,500-1,580, surface ocean 970-1,020, atmosphere 750 (~0.04% CO2, ~420 ppm), land biota 610; photosynthesis 6CO2 + 6H2O + light -> C6H12O6 + 6O2; respiration reverses; ocean sink absorbs about a quarter to half of emitted CO2 (cold water absorbs more); biological pump; burial as CaCO3 (forams, coccolithophores, corals) and organic matter; fossil fuel ~10 GT/yr. NITROGEN: air 78% N2 (largest pool, unusable): N FIXATION (N2 -> NH3/NH4+; Trichodesmium and other cyanobacteria, Rhizobium in legume nodules, Azotobacter, lightning, Haber-Bosch fertilizer [1913; ~70% of reactive N now human-made]), ASSIMILATION (plants/phytoplankton take up NO3-/NH4+), AMMONIFICATION (decomposers: organic N -> NH4+), NITRIFICATION (aerobic: NH4+ -> NO2- [Nitrosomonas] -> NO3- [Nitrobacter]; NH4+ + 2O2 -> NO3- + 2H+ + H2O; consumes O2 and alkalinity, lowers pH; inhibited <pH 6), DENITRIFICATION (anaerobic: NO3- -> N2; also anammox); ocean limiting nutrient usually N (nitrate main form), freshwater usually P, HNLC regions (Southern Ocean, equatorial Pacific, N Pacific) Fe-limited (iron fertilization -> bloom, CO2 drawdown); NH3 (toxic, rises with pH and temp) vs NH4+; nitrite unstable, low; nitrate soluble, leaches, MCL 10 mg/L as N (blue-baby = methemoglobinemia), unpolluted <1 ppm. PHOSPHORUS: no atmospheric phase (sedimentary cycle, slowest); stored in rock/sediment (apatite), released by weathering -> rivers; orthophosphate PO4 3- is the bioavailable form; in DNA/RNA, ATP, phospholipids; binds soil (leaches less than nitrate); guano returns marine P to land; ocean P ends in sediment (uplift recycles over millions of yr); sources: fertilizer, detergents, sewage, manure, erosion; total P = ortho + condensed + organic. SILICON: diatom frustules, radiolarians, sponges; rivers main source, dams trap it; deep water silicate-rich. SULFUR: sulfate main form; sulfate reducers make H2S (rotten egg, anoxic mud); DMS from phytoplankton -> cloud nuclei; SO2/NOx -> acid rain. Redfield C:N:P = 106:16:1 (atoms; N:P 7:1 by weight); (CH2O)106(NH3)16H3PO4; AOU = O2 saturation - measured (respiration measure). Remineralization at depth -> deep water rich in nutrients/CO2, low O2; only upwelling returns them (so productive coasts). Chemosynthesis: 14H2S + 6CO2 + O2 -> C6H12O6 + 8H2O + 14S; nitrifiers, sulfur oxidizers; vent/seep/whale-fall food webs. Detritus (solid dead matter) vs DOM (dissolved; viral lysis largest source) -> microbial loop; marine snow/fecal pellets carry C to depth (~5% of surface production reaches deep sea).

6. EUTROPHICATION, HABs, SEDIMENT, POLLUTION: Cultural eutrophication: N + P (fertilizer, sewage, manure, detergents, urban runoff, atmospheric NOx) -> algal/cyanobacterial blooms -> turbidity, shading (SAV/seagrass/coral loss, epiphytic diatoms decline) -> bloom dies, sinks -> bacteria decompose and consume bottom O2 -> HYPOXIA (<2 mg/L; anoxia = 0; H2S, black sediment) -> fish/benthos kills = DEAD ZONES (~150-400+ worldwide; Gulf of Mexico, Chesapeake, Baltic; warm, stratified summer; living algae release O2 - DECOMPOSERS use it); effects: decreased transparency (NOT increased), lower DO/diversity, higher BOD/turbidity/temp; oligotrophic < mesotrophic < eutrophic < hypereutrophic (Carlson TSI = 60 - 14.41 ln(SD): <40 oligo, 40-50 meso, 50-70 eutro, >70 hyper); remedies: buffer strips/riparian vegetation, wetlands (denitrify), cover crops, fertilizer limits, tertiary N/P removal, septic care, low-P detergent. HABs (harmful algal blooms; "red tide" is not tide-related; colors red/brown/green; ~6% of phytoplankton spp. form them): cause = nutrients (high N:P, lost silica -> diatoms shift to dinoflagellates), warm calm stratified water, runoff, ballast-water cysts; toxins bioaccumulate in filter feeders (shellfish) and biomagnify: Karenia brevis (Gulf of Mexico/Florida) brevetoxin -> neurotoxic shellfish poisoning NSP + fish kills + aerosol respiratory irritation, manatee kills; Alexandrium saxitoxin -> PSP paralytic; Pseudo-nitzschia (diatom) domoic acid -> ASP amnesic (seabirds, sea lions); Dinophysis okadaic acid -> DSP diarrhetic; Gambierdiscus (on seaweed/turf) ciguatoxin -> CIGUATERA (biomagnifies: herbivores -> grouper, snapper, barracuda, jacks; GI + neuro: hot/cold reversal; no reliable test); Pfiesteria (fish sores, memory loss); Microcystis/cyanobacteria microcystin, anatoxin, nodularin (freshwater/Lake Erie); azaspiracid; Noctiluca bioluminescent; dogs more susceptible; dinoflagellate cysts seed blooms (cyst -> excystment -> multiplication -> biomass); damage = toxins, gill clogging, hypoxia on decay, shading. SEDIMENTATION pollution: sources construction, farming/tillage, logging, mining, dredging, stream-bank erosion, urban runoff; effects: turbidity (less light), smothers benthos/eggs/coral/oyster reefs, clogs gills and filter feeders, warms water (absorbs heat), carries nutrients/metals/pathogens, fills channels/reservoirs; largest NONPOINT pollutant by volume; natural ~30% vs accelerated ~70% of erosion; controls: silt fences, riparian buffers, retention basins, no-till, cover crops, revegetation, mangrove/marsh protection. Grain sizes: gravel >2 mm, sand 2-0.06, silt 0.06-0.004, clay <0.004 mm; mud = silt + clay; fine sediments carry the pollutants; flocculation in salt water. EMERGING CONTAMINANTS: PFAS (per-/poly-fluoroalkyl substances; "forever chemicals": PFOA, PFOS, GenX, PFNA, PFBS; strong C-F bond, resist heat/water/oil/degradation; nonstick/Teflon, firefighting foam (AFFF), stain-proofing, food packaging; bioaccumulate in blood/liver/fish, travel in water (don't bind soil well); health: high cholesterol, thyroid disease, kidney/testicular cancer, liver damage, immune suppression, low birth weight, pregnancy hypertension; cross placenta/breast milk; US EPA 2024 limit 4 ppt PFOA & PFOS; removal by granular activated carbon, ion exchange, RO, not conventional treatment). Pharmaceuticals/personal-care products (PPCPs; antibiotics, antidepressants, hormones/EE2, painkillers, sunscreen oxybenzone/octinoxate [coral bleaching]) = endocrine disruption (feminized fish), antibiotic resistance; pass through WWTPs partly; don't flush meds (drug take-back); microplastics/microbeads. OTHER POLLUTION: Biodegradable waste (sewage/manure: bacteria up, DO down); nutrient; thermal (power-plant cooling water [point], urban pavement runoff, dam hypolimnetic release, shade loss: warm water holds less O2, raises metabolism, disease, bleaching); chemical/toxic (pesticides: DDT/PCBs persistent, eggshell thinning in brown pelican, banned 1972/1979; heavy metals: Hg [Minamata; methylmercury made by bacteria, biomagnifies in tuna/swordfish], Pb, Cd, As; TBT imposex in dogwhelks); radioactive; oil (only ~5% from tanker spills; most from runoff/small boats/ships; fresh oil toxic aromatics; Exxon Valdez 1989; weathering -> tar balls; booms/skimmers/bioremediation, dispersants questionable; persists in marsh/mangrove sediment decades); plastics & ghost nets; noise; invasive species; overfishing (MSY = rK/4; bycatch ~25% of catch; drift nets; destructive gear; Magnuson-Stevens; EEZ 200 nm; quotas, size/mesh limits, MPAs [seeding + spillover]); climate change (ocean warms, sea level +3.1 mm/yr, acidification, deoxygenation, species shift poleward). LAWS: Clean Water Act 1972 (NPDES permit for point sources into navigable waters; secondary treatment minimum; TMDL), Safe Drinking Water Act 1974 (MCLs), MARPOL, MMPA 1972, CITES.

7. POTABLE & WASTEWATER TREATMENT: Sewage ~99.9% water. PRELIMINARY/PRIMARY (physical): bar screen -> grit chamber -> primary clarifier (settles ~50-60% SS, ~30% BOD; sludge; skim FOG fats-oils-grease); SECONDARY (biological): aeration tank/activated sludge (microbial floc oxidizes BOD; air + mixing), trickling filter, oxidation ditch; secondary clarifier; return activated sludge (RAS) recycled to aeration, waste activated sludge (WAS) removed; ~85-95% BOD removal; low F/M or low DO -> filamentous bulking; effluent standard BOD5 30 mg/L, TSS 30 mg/L (monthly avg); TERTIARY/advanced: N removal (nitrify aerobic then denitrify anoxic), P removal (alum/ferric/lime precipitation, biological), sand/membrane filtration, activated carbon (adsorption), DISINFECTION (chlorine most common; Cl2/hypochlorite kills >99% but dechlorinate; UV; ozone; chloramine); sludge: anaerobic digestion (acid formers -> methanogens; methane), dewatering, land application/incineration/landfill; sludge dumping at sea banned; combined sewers overflow (CSO) untreated flow in storms (sanitary + stormwater) = major source of bacteria/viruses to estuaries; separate storm sewers. Primary + secondary in nearly all municipal plants; US raw BOD5 ~110-400 mg/L (typical 200-220), household septic 50-150. SEPTIC: tank (anaerobic digestion, solids settle, scum floats) -> drain field (soil bacteria finish); fails if not pumped/poor drainage -> fecal coliform, nitrate, P to groundwater/estuary. POTABLE (conventional): intake/screen -> coagulation (alum Al2(SO4)3 or ferric salts neutralize negative colloids; rapid mix) -> flocculation (slow mix, flocs grow; polymers) -> sedimentation -> filtration (sand/anthracite; slow sand filter = schmutzdecke biofilm, needs low turbidity) -> disinfection (chlorine/chloramine/UV/ozone) -> fluoridation -> storage; softening (lime-soda), aeration (Fe/Mn, gases), activated carbon (taste/odor/organics), membranes (micro-/ultra-/nano-filtration, RO), desalination (reverse osmosis most popular, distillation, electrodialysis, freezing; brine disposal problem); coagulation removes turbidity; algal blooms stress sedimentation/filtration and add taste/odor + toxins; direct potable reuse needs advanced treatment; groundwater needs little treatment (low turbidity, hard).

PART III - PARAMETERS, TESTS, RELATIONSHIPS (know units, healthy ranges, causes, effects, graphs): SALINITY: ppt/psu/% ; seawater 35 ppt (34-37), estuary 0.5-30 brackish; measured by hydrometer (density/buoyancy; floats HIGHER in denser saltier water; T-sensitive), refractometer (refractive index rises with salt; 0-100 ppt), conductivity meter/salinometer/CTD (conductivity, T-corrected), chloride titration (AgNO3: S = 1.80655 x Cl); freshwater <0.5; salinity lowers DO solubility, raises density/conductivity, drives estuary stratification; high salinity from evaporation, brine, road salt, seawater intrusion, fertilizer, wastes; stunts plants, dehydrates freshwater organisms. TEMPERATURE: thermometer/probe; ocean -2 to 30C; warm water less dense (stratifies), holds LESS O2/CO2, raises metabolic and decomposition rates, photosynthesis, disease/parasite/toxin sensitivity, bleaching (+1-2C above summer max), HAB growth; direct with nutrients/BOD/coliform growth; inverse with DO, pH(slightly), gas solubility; thermal pollution (cooling water, runoff from hot pavement, shade removal, dam release, turbidity). pH: -log[H+]; scale 0-14, log (1 unit = 10x; 2 units = 100x); seawater ~8.1 (8.0-8.3), rain 5.6 (acid rain <5.0-5.6; SO2/NOx), streams optimal 6.5-8.5, natural waters 5-8.5; pH 8 -> 7.9 = +26% [H+]; pH probe/meter, paper; buffered by carbonate system CO2 + H2O <-> H2CO3 <-> H+ + HCO3- <-> 2H+ + CO3 2-; photosynthesis (uses CO2) raises pH by day (8.9), respiration lowers it by night (7.2); CO2 pH-lowering; limestone raises. ALKALINITY = acid-neutralizing capacity (HCO3-, CO3 2-, OH-). ARAGONITE SATURATION Omega = [Ca2+][CO3 2-]/Ksp: Omega = 1 saturated; >1 supersaturated (CaCO3 forms); <1 undersaturated (dissolves); coral reef growth: >4 optimal, 3.5-4 adequate, 3-3.5 marginal, <3 poor; highest in warm low-latitude surface water (~4 near equator; W coast S. America ~3), lowest in cold polar water and deep water; ocean acidification: CO2 + H2O -> H2CO3 -> H+ + HCO3-; H+ + CO3 2- -> HCO3- (uses up carbonate) -> pH down, carbonate down, Omega DOWN (so shells/skeletons of corals, mollusks, pteropods, coccolithophores weaken; aragonite saturation horizon shoals; maps of 2000 vs 2050: high-Omega blue zone shrinks/disappears; ~30% more acidic since 1750 (pH -0.1), cold water absorbs more CO2). Ocean absorbs ~25-30% of emitted CO2; anthropogenic CO2 + fossil fuels cause it; limit by cutting CO2. PHOSPHATE (PO4 3-): <0.03 ppm good (0.005-0.05 natural); ascorbic acid/molybdate colorimetry (test kits); sources fertilizers, detergents, sewage, manure, erosion, industrial, boiler additives; spurs algae -> low DO. NITRATE (NO3-): <1 ppm good, MCL 10 mg/L as N; forms NH3/NH4+, NO2-, NO3-; sources fertilizer, septic, manure, sewage plants, storm drains, exhaust; test kits/probes/spectrophotometer; excess -> blooms; methemoglobinemia. TURBIDITY: NTU (nephelometric: scattered light at 90 deg); Secchi disk (20 cm black/white; avg of disappearance + reappearance depth, cm/m; deeper = clearer; photic depth ~2-3x Secchi; not for fast shallow water), turbidity tube, Jackson candle (JTU); causes: sediment, plankton blooms, dead organics, tannins, resuspension, runoff, mining; effects: less photosynthesis, clog gills, warm water, carry pathogens/toxins, shield microbes from disinfection; clear <5 NTU (visible >5); estuaries more turbid/variable, shallower photic zone than open ocean; low-turbidity best with nephelometry, high with transmitted light; salt flocculates clay (clearer). DISSOLVED OXYGEN: mg/L (ppm) or % saturation (air 21% = 210,000 ppm); surface waters 5-15 ppm; >6 good, <5 stress, 1-2 ppm few hours kills large fish, <2 hypoxic, 0 anoxic; >110% saturation can harm; sources: diffusion, wind/turbulence/aeration, photosynthesis; sinks: respiration, decomposition, nitrification, BOD, warm/salty water; diel cycle: min at dawn, max mid/late afternoon; algae bloom causes big swings; measured by Winkler titration (Mn(OH)2 fixes O2; acid frees I2; titrate with thiosulfate: O2 + 4S2O3 2- + 4H+ -> 2S4O6 2- + 2H2O; 1 mol O2 = 4 mol thiosulfate), DO probe/kit; cold (0C ~14.6 mg/L fresh, 20C ~9.1, 30C ~7.6; 35 ppt 20C ~7.4); DO guide: >9 ppm optimal, 7-8 acceptable, 3.5-6 poor, <3.5 fatal. BOD: O2 consumed by microbes decomposing organic matter; BOD5 = 5 days at 20C in the DARK; BOD = DOi - DOf (undiluted); diluted: BOD5 = (DOi - DOf)/P, P = sample volume/bottle volume (50 mL in 300 mL: 7.60 -> 3.80 = 3.80 x 6 = 22.8 mg/L; 20 mL in 300, 9.7 -> 1.2 = 127.5; 10 mL in 150, 7.5 -> 1.4 = 6.1 x 15 = 91.5); seeded: BOD = [(D1 - D2) - (B1 - B2)f]/P; <5 ppm clean (1-2 excellent), 5-10 moderate, >10 polluted; untreated sewage ~200 mg/L; secondary effluent ~30; BOD up -> DO down (inverse); BOD rises with temperature, nutrients, organic waste, algae die-off; also COD (chemical, dichromate). TOTAL SOLIDS: total = dissolved (TDS: pass filter; Ca, HCO3, Na, Cl, N, P, Fe, S; conductivity ~ TDS x 0.5-0.7) + suspended (TSS: retained by filter; silt, clay, plankton, organics); gravimetric: mg/L = (mass residue mg)/(volume L) (120 mg in 250 mL = 480 mg/L; TDS 30.12 - 30.00 g/0.50 L = 0.24 g/L; TSS (650-100 mg)/0.5 L = 1100 mg/L); sources runoff, erosion, dredging, mining, road salt, fertilizers, sewage plants, decay; effects: turbidity, heat absorption, bind toxins/metals, osmotic effects, eutrophication; TDS limit 500 mg/L (secondary). FECAL COLIFORM: gut bacteria of warm-blooded animals; indicator of fecal contamination (not usually pathogens themselves; disease = typhoid, dysentery, gastroenteritis, hepatitis A, ear infections, cholera); counted as colonies (CFU) or MPN per 100 mL; >200/100 mL = swimming limit exceeded (pathogens likely); shellfish waters 14/100 mL; drinking 0; marine enterococci ~35 geometric mean/104 single; membrane filtration (0.45 um filter on selective medium, 44.5C) or multiple-tube MPN (lactose broth, gas in Durham tube); sources failing septic, WWTP/CSO discharge, livestock/manure, wildlife, pets; grows faster in warm, nutrient-rich water; total coliform > fecal coliform > E. coli (nested). RELATIONSHIPS: temp up -> DO down, BOD/nutrient/coliform/TDS growth up, Omega/CO2 solubility down; nutrients (N,P) up -> algae up -> turbidity up, DO swings, then BOD up, DO down at depth, pH day-high/night-low; sewage/manure -> coliform, BOD, nitrate, phosphate, turbidity all up, DO down; salinity up -> density, conductivity up, DO down; photosynthesis -> O2 up, CO2 down, pH up; respiration/decomposition -> O2 down, CO2 up, pH down; storms -> turbidity, TSS, coliform, nutrients up; CO2 up -> pH down, Omega down. DATA TIPS: read units/axes; find baseline vs impacted site; farm/urban runoff signature = nitrate, phosphate, coliform, BOD, turbidity up + DO down; hot-water discharge = temp up, DO down only; saltwater intrusion = conductivity/chloride up; acid input = pH down, Omega down; sewage plant = coliform, BOD, ammonia, phosphate. Macroinvertebrates: EPT (mayfly, stonefly, caddisfly) = intolerant/clean; midge larvae, tubifex worms, leeches, polychaete Capitella, snails = tolerant/organic pollution; higher richness/evenness = healthier; marine/estuary indicators: oysters/clams, polychaetes, amphipods, crabs; biotic and saprobic indices; Shannon.

PART IV - SALINOMETER/HYDROMETER (10%): Goal: read NaCl 1-10% (m/v; g per 100 mL; 3.5% = seawater; 10% = 100 g/L) in a 400-600 mL beaker (>=400 mL) to nearest 0.1% (+/-1% regionals, +/-0.5% states). Hydrometer = sealed float with ballast (shot) at bottom, narrow graduated stem; floats HIGHER in denser (saltier) water (Archimedes: weight = weight of displaced fluid); sensitivity increases with narrower stem, longer stem, lighter weight; need stem long enough for 1-10% (density 1.005-1.070 g/mL at 20C: 1% 1.0053, 2% 1.0125, 3% 1.0196, 3.5% 1.0232, 4% 1.0268, 5% 1.0340, 6% 1.0413, 8% 1.0559, 10% 1.0707; ~0.72% density per 1% salt; density falls ~0.2-0.3 kg/m3 per C). Build from straw/pipette/test tube with clay, BBs or nails; vertical float, no wall contact, bubbles/dirt off. Calibrate: make known standards (e.g., 1, 2, 3.5, 5, 7.5, 10% by weighing NaCl to make 100 mL or by mass), same temperature as test, mark waterline for each, draw graph (mark position vs %) and use LINEAR interpolation/curve; check with distilled (0%); read at the meniscus at eye level; stir gently, let settle, repeat 3x and average; re-zero between samples (rinse, dry); keep temperature steady (density changes with T). Electronic: conductivity probe (no programmable parts): conductance rises with salt (curves at high %); calibrate with standards, temperature-correct, same electrode depth/spacing; ~50-54 mS/cm at 35 ppt (25C). Conversions: % m/v x 10 = g/L; ppt ~ g/kg; 35 ppt ~ 3.5%; 1% = 10 ppt.

PART II - CORAL REEF ECOLOGY: Reefs = rock mounds/ridges of CaCO3 (limestone) deposited by organisms; living reef = thin top veneer; largest structures built by organisms; shelter/food/breeding sites, breakwater/storm protection; ~25% of marine species on <1% of ocean floor ("rainforests of the sea"); GBR >2,000 km long (2,500+ reefs, ~400 coral spp, 1,500 fish spp; largest reef structure; Red Sea fringing reef longest continuous ~4,000 km); services: fisheries/nurseries, tourism, coastal protection, medicines (bioactive compounds), biodiversity. Phylum Cnidaria, class Anthozoa (polyp only; sea anemone relatives; 6-fold septa/tentacles in stony corals, 8-fold in octocorals). STONY/HARD (scleractinian) = main reef architects: each polyp secretes aragonite CaCO3 cup (corallite); colony = polyps linked by coenosarc tissue sheet sharing gastrovascular canals + nerve net; living tissue thin, skeleton bulk; polyp = tentacles with nematocysts around mouth (only opening; mouth also expels waste), gastrovascular cavity, mesenteries/mesenterial filaments (digest externally, bear gonads), calicoblastic basal tissue + skeleton; growth forms: branching (fast, shades others), plate/foliose, massive/brain (slow, aggressive via mesenterial filaments + sweeper tentacles), columnar, encrusting, free-living (mushroom coral Fungia); reefs grow ~1-2 cm/yr (branching up to 10+ cm); skeleton growth bands like tree rings record SST/salinity/runoff history. HERMATYPIC = reef-building, large colonies, zooxanthellae; AHERMATYPIC = solitary/small colonies, usually no zooxanthellae (deep/cold-water corals, to >6,000 m). Other reef builders: crustose coralline red algae (CaCO3 "cement", algal ridge; also settlement cue), Halimeda (calcareous green alga; reef sand), fire coral Millepora (hydrozoan), blue coral Heliopora, organ-pipe Tubipora, worm reefs (Phragmatopoma), oysters; non-builders: soft corals, gorgonians. ZOOXANTHELLAE (Symbiodiniaceae) = golden-brown dinoflagellate algae (NOT bacteria) in coral tissue: give up to ~90% of coral nutrition (glucose, glycerol, amino acids, O2) and color; coral gives shelter, CO2, N and P wastes, protection (mutualism - obligate for reef building; nutrients recycled so reefs thrive in nutrient-poor "oligotrophic" water = oasis in a desert). Coral also feeds by nematocyst-armed tentacles (zooplankton, mostly night), mucus nets, mesenterial filaments, dissolved organic matter. Requirements: hard substrate; warm 23-29C (>20C min; upper ~30-35C); clear shallow water/high light (rarely below 50 m); oceanic salinity 32-42 ppt (no river mouths); low nutrients; wave action/circulation (food, O2, removes sediment); Omega >3-4; 30N-30S, W sides of ocean basins (warm currents) - fewer reefs on W coasts of continents (cold upwelling: Benguela, Peru). Regions: Indo-Pacific (largest; most species; Coral Triangle = Indonesia, Philippines, Malaysia, PNG, Timor-Leste, Solomon Is.), Red Sea, Greater Caribbean/W Atlantic (fewer species: Isthmus of Panama closed 3 Mya + ice-age extinctions). Reef TYPES (Darwin sequence youngest -> oldest, sinking volcano): FRINGING (attached to shore; reef flat/crest/slope) -> BARRIER (offshore, separated by deep lagoon; GBR, Belize) -> ATOLL (ring + central lagoon, no island; mostly Indo-Pacific; Enewetak drilling proved >1,400 m limestone over volcano) -> seamount/guyot; patch reef = isolated lagoon platform. ZONES: reef flat/back reef (shallow, sand/seagrass, hardest conditions) -> reef crest (breaks waves; algal ridge; stout corals) -> fore/outer reef slope (most coral and fish diversity ~10-30 m, spur-and-groove). Coral life cycle: adult colonies broadcast-spawn gamete bundles (mass spawning a few nights after full moon, warm water, after dusk; external fertilization) or brood (internal; larger planulae with zooxanthellae) -> planula (ciliated swimming larva, no zooxanthellae in most broadcasters) settles on hard clean surface (cued by coralline algae) -> metamorphoses into founding polyp -> divides/buds -> colony; asexual: budding, fission, FRAGMENTATION (storm pieces; basis of coral gardening/microfragmentation restoration = fast but low genetic diversity); ~75% of species hermaphroditic broadcast spawners. Reef food web: producers = zooxanthellae, turf/coralline/fleshy algae, cyanobacteria (N-fixers); grazers (parrotfish, surgeonfish, urchins, chitons, snails); coral/mucus eaters (butterflyfish, crown-of-thorns, parrotfish, snails, filefish); detritus feeders (sea cucumbers, worms); plankton feeders (damselfish, fans, feather stars, Christmas-tree worms); predators (groupers, snappers, moray, triton); cleaners; bioerosion (parrotfish, urchins' Aristotle's lantern, boring sponges Cliona, clams, worms): reef grows only if accretion > erosion; damselfish farm algae gardens. HEALTH INDICATORS: apex predators, fish biomass, average catch length, % live coral cover, Hard Coral Index = HC/(HC + recently killed coral), indicator organisms. REEF CHECK (volunteer survey since 1996; 1997-2001 ~1,500 reefs) indicator -> threat: OVERFISHING = grouper >30 cm, snapper, sweetlips (Haemulidae), parrotfish >20 cm, moray eel, lobster (also humphead wrasse, bumphead parrotfish, barramundi cod, Nassau grouper); AQUARIUM FISH COLLECTING = butterflyfish, banded coral shrimp; CURIO COLLECTION = triton, pencil urchin, giant clam (flamingo tongue snail shell); NUTRIENT POLLUTION = fleshy algae (and sponge overabundance); Diadema = grazing balance (HIGH = overfishing of urchin predators/triggerfish; LOW = Caribbean 1983 die-off or herbivore overfishing); crown-of-thorns = outbreak risk (nutrients, predator loss); blast fishing reduces haemulids/parrotfish/coral; cyanide poisoning (live reef fish + aquarium trade) kills coral. THREATS: climate change (bleaching, acidification, storms, sea-level rise), overfishing/destructive fishing (dynamite, cyanide), pollution (nutrients, sewage, chemicals, sunscreen oxybenzone/octinoxate, oil), sedimentation (dredging, deforestation, construction; smothers, blocks light), coastal development/mangrove loss, tourism (anchors: one cruise-ship anchor destroyed ~half a football field, recovery ~50 yr; trampling, souvenirs), aquarium trade, coral mining, disease, crown-of-thorns, invasives; ~25-50% reefs lost or at high risk; >50% of reef-building coral cover gone since 1950s. BLEACHING: stress (heat +1-2C above summer max for >=4 weeks, high light, low salinity, sediment, pollution, cold, disease, acidification) -> coral expels zooxanthellae -> transparent tissue shows white skeleton; coral survives days-weeks, can recover if stress ends and algae return (starves/dies if prolonged); NOAA Degree Heating Weeks (1 DHW = 1 wk at 1C above max monthly mean): watch (0<HotSpot<1), warning (HotSpot>=1, DHW<4: possible bleaching), Alert 1 (4<=DHW<8: reef-wide bleaching), Alert 2 (8-12: bleaching + mortality), 3 (12-16 multi-species mortality), 4 (16-20: >50% die), 5 (>=20: >80% die); peak season July-Sept northern hemisphere, Jan-Mar southern; mass bleaching 1982-83, 1998, 2014-17 (GBR 2016 + 2017 back-to-back), 2023-25; El Nino raises SST; sunscreen chemicals bleach coral. RECENTLY KILLED CORAL = coral that died within the past year (white skeleton, no turf algae yet; becomes dead coral with turf/macroalgae after ~1 yr). COTS outbreaks, disease (white band: Acropora palmata/cervicornis; black band: cyanobacteria ring; yellow band/blotch; white plague/white pox; Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease SCTLD (Florida 2014-; >20 stony spp; brain/pillar corals die fast; amoxicillin paste treatment); aspergillosis of sea fans (fungus Aspergillus sydowii; purple lesions); tissue loss often follows bleaching/warming/poor water quality. Phase shift = fishing + nutrients + lost grazers -> macroalgae dominate, hard to reverse. MANAGEMENT: marine protected areas (reserves: more/bigger fish, spillover + larval seeding), fisheries regulation (size/gear limits, closures, no blast/cyanide), coastal zoning, watershed/runoff control, wastewater treatment, mooring buoys, sustainable tourism, restoration (coral nurseries, outplanting), reducing CO2, TEK-based LMMAs, certified aquarium trade (MAC).
11. ORGANISM CARDS - ID by common name; Reef Check (RC) indicator meaning; GLOBAL unless noted. BANDED CORAL SHRIMP (Stenopus hispidus; Decapoda; RC: AQUARIUM COLLECTING, ~$20/pair): red body with white bands, 2 pairs LONG WHITE antennae, 3 pairs of pincered legs (3rd pair biggest), ~6 cm, female larger; hides in caves/ledges by day (2-210 m); largest CLEANER shrimp: waves antennae + body "dances" at cleaning stations, eats fish parasites/dead tissue/fungus -> MUTUALISM (clients moray, grouper, tang); MONOGAMOUS pairs (not polygamous), territorial; gonochoric, mates right after female molts, eggs on swimmerets (pleopods) hatch ~2 wk at night, NINE planktonic larval stages (~4-8 months, teleplanic -> circumtropical); claws autotomize; molts every 3-8 wk; Atlantic ~10x more than Indo-Pacific. BUTTERFLYFISH (Chaetodon spp., Chaetodontidae; RC: AQUARIUM/ORNAMENTAL TRADE, also eaten): deep laterally compressed disc, small terminal mouth with brush teeth, eye-band + FALSE EYESPOT near tail, no preopercle spine (angelfish have one), rounded tail; ~114-129 spp, 9-30 cm; ~78% eat coral polyps (OBLIGATE corallivores decline with coral loss/bleaching/COTS = coral-health INDICATOR); also plankton, anemones, worms, gorgonians; DIURNAL, hide at night with faded color; monogamous PAIRS defend coral heads; gonochoric, pair spawn at dusk (pelagic buoyant eggs hatch ~1 day, no parental care), larva = tholichthys (bony plates), lunar timing; Indo-Pacific ~10x more spp than Atlantic; coral-feeders nearly impossible in aquaria. CROWN-OF-THORNS STARFISH (Acanthaster planci complex; Asteroidea; Indo-Pacific origin, listed global; RC/no formal indicator): 7-23 arms covered in 4-5 cm venomous spines (saponins), 25-35 cm (to 80), purple/blue/red/green; EVERTS STOMACH over coral polyps (external digestion, leaves white skeleton; prefers Acropora/Pocillopora; ~6-10 m2 coral/yr); gonochoric broadcast spawner (up to tens of millions of eggs; r-selected, Type III), bipinnaria -> brachiolaria larvae (settle on coralline algae; juveniles eat algae ~6 months then switch to coral); lives ~5-8 yr; OUTBREAKS (>15/ha; GBR 4 since 1960s) from nutrient runoff (more phytoplankton for larvae) + loss of predators (triton, humphead wrasse, triggerfish, pufferfish, harlequin shrimp); regenerates when cut (cutting fails); control by injection (bile salts, vinegar), COTSbot; native to GBR (not invasive there). FLESHY ALGAE (macroalgae: Rhodophyta red, Chlorophyta green, Phaeophyta brown; RC: NUTRIENT POLLUTION): red = chl a + phycoerythrin/phycocyanin, floridean starch, agar/carrageenan, no flagella (Porphyra, Gracilaria, Laurencia; coralline reds deposit CaCO3 = cement, settlement cue); green = chl a + b, starch (Ulva sea lettuce, Enteromorpha, Dictyosphaeria bubble alga, Caulerpa; Halimeda = calcified sand-maker); brown = chl a + c + FUCOXANTHIN, laminarin, alginate (Sargassum, Turbinaria, Padina, kelps; seasonal Sargassum not counted); primary producers/food for grazers but overgrow, shade and smother coral, block larval settlement -> CORAL-ALGAL PHASE SHIFT when nutrients rise and herbivores (parrotfish, Diadema) fall; thallus = blade, stipe, holdfast (not a root), pneumatocyst; Fleshy Macroalgal Index = area x height. GROUPER >30 cm (Epinephelinae/Epinephelidae; RC: OVERFISHING of all types): stout, huge mouth with protruding lower jaw, rounded tail, mottled/barred, color change; giant grouper 2.7 m/400 kg, goliath 2.5 m, Nassau 1.2 m; ambush SUCTION feeders (fish, crabs, spiny lobster, octopus), solitary, territorial; COOPERATIVE hunting with moray eels/humphead wrasse (head-shake signal); most PROTOGYNOUS (harems), SPAWNING AGGREGATIONS at fixed sites (easy to fish out), pelagic eggs; slow growth/late maturity; juveniles in seagrass/mangrove; ciguatera carriers; live reef food-fish trade; 48% of 1,022 reefs had none; higher inside MPAs. HARD/STONY CORAL (Scleractinia; RC: % live hard coral cover, Hard Coral Index, recently killed coral): colonial polyps in aragonite cups (septa in multiples of 6 -> hexacoral; octocorals have 8), growth forms branching (Acropora, 10 cm/yr), massive/brain/boulder (Porites, Orbicella, 0.3-2 cm/yr), plate, mushroom (Fungia solitary); zooxanthellae mutualism (up to ~90% of nutrition), also carnivorous (nematocysts at night, mucus nets, mesenterial filaments, DOM); hermaphroditic colonies, mass broadcast spawning after full moon -> planula -> settles (CCA cue) -> polyp; brooders; budding/fragmentation; threatened by COTS, Drupella snails, disease, bleaching, acidification, sediment, nutrients, blast/cyanide fishing, anchors; all Scleractinia CITES II; best reefs only ~32% cover; cover lower with sewage (28 vs 35%), blast (25 vs 35%), poison (21 vs 32%). LOBSTERS (rules: Nephropidae clawed lobsters; Reef Check spiny lobsters Panulirus; RC: OVERFISHING): clawed = huge crusher + cutter pincers, short antennae, cold-temperate (Homarus), omnivore/scavenger, eats shed shell, molts, females carry eggs ~9-11 months, zoea -> postlarva; spiny (Palinuridae) = no claws, long thick spiny antennae, stridulate, NOCTURNAL (den by day), single-file queue marches, phyllosoma larvae 6-12 months -> puerulus; both tail-flip (caridoid escape), eaten by cod/flounder/eel/seal/nurse shark/octopus/snapper (NOT tuna); control urchins/molluscs (loss -> Diadema boom); easy to net/trap/spear; none on 83% of reefs. LONG-SPINED BLACK SEA URCHIN (Diadema antillarum Atlantic; D. setosum/savignyi Indo-Pacific; RC: grazing balance - HIGH = overfished predators/triggerfish, LOW = Caribbean die-off or overfished herbivores): jet-black test, VERY long thin brittle hollow needle spines (10-30 cm, 3-4x test), blue iridescent spots; nocturnal grazer scraping algae (turf, macroalgae, coral recruits) with ARISTOTLE'S LANTERN (5 teeth); hides in crevices by day; bioeroder (~5 kg CaCO3/m2/yr, ~1 cm reef erosion/yr); gonochoric broadcast spawner (monthly near full moon, pheromone-synchronized, echinopluteus larva ~4-6 wk), Type III; 1983-84 waterborne pathogen killed >97% of Caribbean Diadema -> algae overgrew coral (Jamaica live coral 30-70% -> 1-10%), slow recovery (Allee effect); 2022 second die-off; eaten by queen triggerfish, spiny lobster, helmet snail; life ~4-8 yr. MORAY EEL (Muraenidae; RC: OVERFISHING, added 2001): scaleless snake-like body, ONE continuous dorsal-caudal-anal fin, NO pectoral/pelvic fins, small round gill openings (open/close mouth to pump water, not aggression), large mouth with sharp teeth (snowflake/zebra blunt crushers); >100 spp, to 2-4 m; lives in a hole; NOCTURNAL ambush predator (poor eyesight, strong SMELL; fish, crabs, octopus); second set of PHARYNGEAL jaws lunge forward to drag prey in; KNOTS itself for leverage; cooperative hunting with groupers/humphead wrasse; CLEANER mutualism with banded coral shrimp/cleaner wrasse; oviparous, external fertilization, transparent LEPTOCEPHALUS larva; ciguatera accumulator; mucus toxin; many bones + large territorial = easy spear target.
PARROTFISH (Scaridae; global; >20 cm counted) - Reef Check: OVERFISHING (also fewer with blast fishing): ~90-100 spp (not >125); stout body, big scales, FUSED tooth plates = parrot-like BEAK; colorful (blue/green/pink); largest herbivorous reef fish; HERBIVORE grazing turf/endolithic algae off dead coral/rock (<1% of bites live coral) -> keeps algae from overgrowing coral; PHARYNGEAL (throat) teeth grind coral rock -> excretes fine white SAND (up to ~90+ kg/yr per fish; beaches); bioerosion; diurnal; sleep in crevices, some secrete a transparent MUCUS COCOON at night (blocks gnathiid isopod parasites, masks scent); swims mainly with pectoral fins; PROTOGYNOUS (female/initial phase -> bright terminal-phase "supermale"; triggered socially, harems; marbled parrotfish exception); pelagic broadcast spawners, tiny eggs, no parental care; fishing removes big males.
PENCIL URCHIN (Eucidaris spp. Atlantic E. tribuloides; Heterocentrotus mamillatus Indo-Pacific; class Echinoidea) - Reef Check: CURIO COLLECTION: few THICK blunt cylindrical pencil-like spines (Mg-calcite; H. mamillatus red, spines to ~10-12 cm; E. tribuloides to ~13 cm test, tan/brown), unlike slender Diadema; shallow (0-50 m), nocturnal omnivorous grazer (algae, sponges, coral), wedges in crevices; prized for spines (shell arrangements, wind chimes, jewelry, aquarium); NOT harvested for roe; gonochoric broadcast spawner (external fertilization), planktonic pluteus larva; larva bilateral -> adult pentaradial; eaten by triggerfish, wrasses; Aristotle's lantern chewing organ; bioeroder.
SEA CUCUMBER (Holothuroidea; global in 2027 rules; Reef Check species Thelenota ananas prickly redfish, Stichopus chloronotus greenfish): leathery soft cylinder, no arms/spines, mouth with 10-30 feeding tentacles (modified tube feet); DEPOSIT FEEDER/detritivore (can process tens of kg sediment/yr; bioturbation, recycles nutrients, locally buffers pH); respires via anus -> RESPIRATORY TREES; defenses: toxic saponins (holothurin), CUVIERIAN TUBULES (sticky threads shot from anus in some Holothuria), EVISCERATION (expels gut, regrows in weeks); slow-moving; pearlfish/emperor shrimp live on or in it (commensalism); mostly dioecious broadcast spawners (external fertilization) -> auricularia larva -> doliolaria -> pentactula; also asexual fission (Stichopus chloronotus, Holothuria atra); harvested/dried as BECHE-DE-MER/trepang (China) -> overfished (T. ananas IUCN Endangered); eaten by triton, fish, crabs.
SNAPPER (Lutjanidae; global) - Reef Check: OVERFISHING: >100 spp; perch-like, pointed snout, large mouth with canine teeth, continuous dorsal fin, forked/emarginate tail; often red/yellow/silver (deeper = redder); most to ~100 m (some 500); nocturnal-crepuscular carnivores (fish, crabs, shrimp, squid); gonochoric, pelagic broadcast spawners in transient spawning aggregations (full moon, dusk; shelf break) -> vulnerable; juveniles in mangrove/seagrass; female mature ~2 yr; red snapper to 100 cm/22 kg/57 yr, IUCN VU; ciguatera risk; Atlantic ~10x more than Indo-Pacific; higher inside MPAs; diet similar to sweetlips.
DEMOSPONGES (Demospongiae; phylum Porifera; global; >90% of sponges, ~8,800 spp): skeleton of SPONGIN (protein) fibers and/or SILICA spicules (calcareous sponges are another class; glass sponges another); no true tissues/organs/nerves; water flows IN through ostia (pores) -> canals -> choanocyte (collar-cell) chambers (flagella create current, trap bacteria/particles) -> OUT the osculum; ACTIVE filter/suspension feeder; amoebocytes (archaeocytes) transport food, make spicules; sessile; cyanobacteria/zooxanthellae symbionts; reproduction: asexual budding, fragmentation, gemmules; sexual: mostly hermaphroditic, sperm broadcast, internal fertilization, parenchymella larva; Cliona boring sponge erodes coral (bioerosion); toxins/chemical defenses (bioactive drugs); roles: filter water, clean organics/N-P-C cycling (sponge loop), habitat; Caribbean sponges bigger/more abundant; sponge overabundance = excess nutrients; stovepipe, barrel, vase, encrusting, carnivorous harp sponge (deep sea, Chondrocladia); bath sponge Spongia.
SWEETLIPS (Haemulidae; global; ~150 spp incl. grunts/margates) - Reef Check: OVERFISHING: thick fleshy lips; spotted/striped robust body (harlequin sweetlips Plectorhinchus chaetodonoides to 72 cm); JUVENILES brown with big cream blotches and a wiggling "DANCING" swim (mimics toxic flatworm), solitary; NOCTURNAL feeders on benthic invertebrates + small fish (hide under ledges by day, schooling); oviparous pelagic spawners; popular food fish, "oblivious" swimming = first speared; Atlantic has ~50x more haemulids (grunts) than Indo-Pacific; hit by blast fishing; diet like snapper/humphead wrasse.
TRITON (Charonia tritonis Indo-Pacific; C. variegata Atlantic; Charoniidae) - Reef Check: CURIO (SHELL) COLLECTION: large spiral shell to ~50-60 cm (cream/brown mottled, orange-brown flames, toothed lip, hairy periostracum, corneous operculum trapdoor); nocturnal carnivore specializing on echinoderms - CROWN-OF-THORNS (immune to its saponins), other sea stars, sea urchins, sea cucumbers; grips prey with foot, paralytic/venomous (acidic) saliva, radula tears it (gonads/gut first); gonochoric (not hermaphrodite), internal fertilization, female guards 500+ egg capsules (~2,000+ eggs each), planktotrophic veligers drift months (teleplanic); rare; shells ~US$30-50; ~10,000 harvested/yr GBR in 1960s -> COTS outbreak hypothesis (debated); protected Queensland; keystone/predator.
BARRAMUNDI COD (Cromileptes altivelis = humpback/panther grouper; Indo-Pacific only): elongate, small pointed head with concave nape hump, small black spots all over (juveniles: white with large round black spots, wobbly head-down swimming); to ~70 cm; 2-40 m; ambush predator of small fish/crustaceans (dawn/dusk); PROTOGYNOUS (all born female, dominant females turn male ~age 3-9); pelagic eggs, no egg guarding; demersal ray-finned (Actinopterygii); live reef food fish trade (HK ~$100/kg) + juvenile aquarium collection; IUCN data deficient (2016).
BUMPHEAD PARROTFISH (Bolbometopon muricatum; Indo-Pacific only): largest parrotfish (to 1.3 m, 46+ kg, ~40 yr); steep VERTICAL bulbous forehead hump, dull uniform green-gray, fully scaled, exposed tooth plates, sexually monochromatic/GONOCHORIC (no color phases); diurnal schools; eats algae + LIVE CORAL, ramming/head-butting, >5 tons CaCO3 per adult per yr (keystone bioeroder, sand); lunar spawning aggregations near new moon; sleeps in groups at night (easy NIGHT SPEARING); juveniles in branching coral/lagoons; IUCN Vulnerable; lookalike humphead wrasse (hump + thick lips + eye lines).
GIANT CLAM (Tridacna spp.; Indo-Pacific only; Bivalvia): T. gigas largest bivalve (~1.2-1.4 m, 200+ kg, can live 100+ yr; others T. squamosa, derasa, maxima, crocea); wavy fluted shell, colorful iridescent MANTLE; MIXOTROPH: zooxanthellae in mantle give ~65-70% of nutrition + FILTER FEEDS (incurrent siphon); bleaches under heat; SIMULTANEOUS HERMAPHRODITE (no self-fertilization: sperm first then eggs), BROADCAST spawner (>500 million eggs), trochophore -> veliger -> pediveliger -> juvenile; needs neighbors (Allee effect); not a man-eater; T. gigas IUCN Critically Endangered (2024), all Tridacnidae CITES II; Reef Check: CURIO + food harvest (adductor muscle); no giant clams in Atlantic/Caribbean; same class as scallops/oysters.
HUMPHEAD (NAPOLEON) WRASSE (Cheilinus undulatus; Indo-Pacific only; Labridae): to ~2 m, 190 kg, ~30 yr; thick lips, forehead HUMP (bigger in males), 2 black lines behind eye + wavy head lines, blue-green; juveniles lack hump; carnivore: mollusks, urchins, crustaceans, fish, toxic prey (sea hares, boxfish) and CROWN-OF-THORNS (natural COTS predator), cooperative hunting with roving coral grouper; PROTOGYNOUS, spawning aggregations (pelagic eggs), matures ~5-7 yr; approaches divers; umbrella species; IUCN ENDANGERED, CITES II; threats live reef food-fish trade (lips), cyanide, night spearing, juveniles collected; Reef Check: OVERFISHING.
FLAMINGO TONGUE SNAIL (Cyphoma gibbosum; Atlantic only; Ovulidae gastropod): ~25-35 mm; shell is plain smooth pale/white with a ridge - the orange-spotted black-ringed pattern is the live MANTLE covering the shell (shell dull white when dead -> over-collected as curio); feeds on live GORGONIANS (sea fans/whips; scrapes polyps with radula, leaves feeding scars, usually not lethal); sequesters toxins -> aposematic (warning) coloration; dioecious, internal fertilization (male on right side of female shell), eggs in capsules on bare gorgonian axis (~300 embryos, hatch ~10 d), planktonic veliger; predators spiny lobster, hogfish, pufferfish (overfishing of them increases snails); threat = shell collection (curio).
GORGONIA / OCTOCORALS (Octocorallia; Atlantic only for G. ventalina sea fan): Anthozoa with EIGHT feathery (pinnate) tentacles per polyp; skeleton = flexible protein GORGONIN axis + calcite SCLERITES (no aragonite cup; not a reef builder); sea fans flat, grow PERPENDICULAR to current (passive suspension feeders, zooplankton at night) and host zooxanthellae; colors purple/yellow/brown (not green); mostly gonochoric broadcast spawners, some brooders, asexual fragmentation; eaten by flamingo tongue snail, butterflyfish, fireworm, nudibranch; ASPERGILLOSIS (fungus Aspergillus sydowii, purple lesions) disease; habitat 3-D structure; drugs (pseudopterosins); souvenir fans.
NASSAU GROUPER (Epinephelus striatus; Atlantic/Caribbean only; Serranidae): stout, large mouth; 5 dark vertical bars, forked "tuning fork" stripe on forehead, black spots near eyes, black saddle at tail base, rapid color change; to >1 m, 25 kg, ~29 yr; juveniles in seagrass/algal clumps, adults reefs/ledges to ~100 m; solitary diurnal ambush predator (fish 54%, crabs, lobster, octopus); mature 4-5 yr; SPAWNING AGGREGATIONS Dec-Jan around full moon at traditional shelf-edge sites (100s-10,000s of fish) -> overfished; protogynous (classically); pelagic eggs; IUCN CRITICALLY ENDANGERED (2018), US ESA Threatened (2016), harvest banned; Reef Check: grouper = OVERFISHING.
12. FAST ANSWERS & TRAPS (what tests keep asking): (1) Coral-zooxanthellae = MUTUALISM; zooxanthellae are dinoflagellate ALGAE. (2) Warm water holds LESS O2; cold holds more; DO lowest at dawn. (3) Salinity up -> density, conductivity up; DO down. (4) Phosphate leaches LESS than nitrate; P has no gas phase. (5) Freshwater usually P-limited, ocean/estuary usually N-limited, HNLC = iron. (6) Ocean acidification LOWERS carbonate and aragonite Omega; Omega >4 optimal, <3 poor, 1 = saturated. (7) r = many/small/short-lived/Type III; K = few/large/long/Type I. (8) Hypoxia <2 mg/L; DECOMPOSERS (not living algae) drain O2. (9) BOD is measured in the DARK, 5 d, 20C; BOD up = DO down. (10) Fecal coliform = indicator (not usually the pathogen); drinking 0/100 mL; swimming 200; shellfish 14. (11) Secchi depth is NOT light depth (photic ~2-3x deeper); deeper Secchi = clearer. (12) pH 5 is 10x more acidic than 6; 100x than 7. (13) Hydrometer = density/buoyancy (floats higher in salty water); salinometer/CTD = conductivity (T-corrected). (14) Coagulation precedes flocculation; slow sand filters need LOW turbidity; primary = physical, secondary = biological, tertiary = nutrient removal/polishing/disinfection. (15) Largest reservoirs: C = sedimentary rock (carbonates), N = atmosphere, P = rock/sediment; 97% water is salt; groundwater = largest liquid fresh store. (16) Estuary types: salt wedge = high river flow; well mixed = strong tides/shallow; fjord = sill + glacier; bar-built = barrier island; coastal plain = drowned river valley (Chesapeake); tectonic = SF Bay; inverse = arid hypersaline. (19) GPGP is dispersed microplastic soup; five patches = N/S Pacific, N/S Atlantic, Indian; none "Central Pacific". (20) Biomagnification goes UP the food chain (fat-soluble persistent: DDT, PCBs, MeHg, PFAS in protein); bioaccumulation is within one organism. (21) Energy pyramids never invert; biomass pyramids can (phytoplankton turnover); 10% rule (50,000 J -> 500 J secondary consumers). (22) Protogynous (female -> male): parrotfish, wrasse (humphead), groupers (Nassau, barramundi cod); protandrous (male -> female): clownfish; giant clam, sponge = simultaneous hermaphrodites; triton, urchins, butterflyfish, bumphead parrotfish = separate sexes. (23) Indo-Pacific only: humphead wrasse, barramundi cod, bumphead parrotfish, giant clam; Atlantic only: Gorgonia, flamingo tongue snail, Nassau grouper; all others global (sea cucumber and demosponge included). (24) Crown-of-thorns eats coral polyps (external stomach digestion); triton and humphead wrasse eat COTS; nutrient runoff boosts COTS larvae. (25) Diadema = grazer; 1983 Caribbean die-off -> algae overgrow coral; pencil urchin = thick blunt spines + curio; Aristotle's lantern = urchin jaw. (26) Banded coral shrimp = cleaner (mutualism), white antennae wave, monogamous, aquarium indicator; moray = nocturnal, pharyngeal jaws, no pectoral fins, knotting. (27) Bleaching = loss of zooxanthellae (not death); needs ~1-2C above summer max; colors come from zooxanthellae + coral pigments. (28) Warm + stratified + nutrient-rich + calm = HAB/bloom conditions; toxins: brevetoxin NSP (Karenia), saxitoxin PSP (Alexandrium), domoic acid ASP (Pseudo-nitzschia diatom), okadaic acid DSP, ciguatoxin (Gambierdiscus; biomagnifies in reef fish), microcystin (freshwater cyanobacteria). (29) Point source = identifiable pipe/outfall; nonpoint = diffuse runoff (most common). (30) PFAS = C-F bond persistence; pharmaceuticals = endocrine disruption/antibiotic resistance; neither is removed well by conventional treatment. (31) TEK = place-based, multigenerational Indigenous knowledge used WITH science (Hawaiian ahupuaa/loko ia, clam gardens, LMMAs, rahui). (32) Largest US dead zone = Gulf of Mexico (Mississippi N+P).
